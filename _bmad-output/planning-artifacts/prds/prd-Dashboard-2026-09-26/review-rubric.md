# PRD Quality Review — Hospital OPD Management System (prd-Dashboard-2026-09-26)

## Overall verdict
This is a good PRD. It has a clear thesis (fair, visible, per-Facility queues fed by a fast triage step, all backed by a persistent Patient Record), domain research that actually drives requirements (RA 9994/10754 priority, DOH ABTC regimen, NPC circulars), dated decisions, and Open Questions that each have an owner. The main risk is the **configuration and administration surface**. The PRD repeatedly says things are "Admin-configurable" (Urgency scale, Priority Categories, Facilities and their hours, prenatal target, display devices, audit review) but none of these has an FR, and one of them (a configurable Urgency scale) contradicts the hard-coded ordering in FR-9. Scope is also unclear in two places: FR-22 against §8.2, and a single-Facility pilot against multi-Facility features. Both need fixing before epics are cut.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions. FR-9 says "Priority is strict … (decided 2026-09-26)", and §11 separates "Resolved on 2026-09-26" from "Still open", with an owner and a revisit point for each open item. The legal basis for each Priority Category is separated from policy ("There is no national law for Pregnant priority"), which is the honest framing an approver needs. The `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions: unverified facility fields (FR-22), DPO review (§6), and go-live approval (§10).

The weak spot is trade-offs. Strict priority was chosen, but the PRD never says what it costs. Under FR-9 a Routine patient at a busy Consultation queue full of seniors has no bound on their wait, and nothing in §9 watches for this. Also, §12 says "The product owner confirmed all of these on 2026-09-26", yet several of those same items are still listed as open in §11. A decision-maker cannot tell which values are settled.

### Findings
- **medium** Strict-priority cost not named (§4.4 FR-9, §9) — "all Urgent and Priority Category entries are served before any Routine entry" is decided, but the alternative that was given up (alternating or capped priority) and its cost (unbounded Routine waits) are not stated. The only fairness metric, SM-4, measures compliance with the rule, not its effect. *Fix:* add one sentence on what was given up, and a counter-metric such as "SM-C3 longest Routine wait per Facility per day", so the hospital can see if strict priority starves Routine patients.
- **medium** "Confirmed" assumptions that are still open (§12 header vs §11 items 1 and 3, FR-7, FR-12) — §12 says all assumptions were confirmed, but "Required Vital Signs and their normal and impossible ranges" and "the end-of-day handling of leftover entries" are listed as still open in §11. FR-7's tag also reads "to be set with the hospital's nursing lead." *Fix:* split §12 into "confirmed defaults" and "pending hospital input", and cross-reference the §11 numbers.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is little furniture here. There are three staff personas plus Patients as indirect users, and each one drives FRs and a UJ. The Vision (§1) is specific to this product: four named Facilities, the Display Board, anti-rabies follow-ups. It could not be swapped into another PRD. The NFRs have thresholds (NFR-1 "under 1 second", NFR-2 "within 5 seconds", NFR-6 "44px touch targets"), and §6 names the actual Philippine instruments with concrete obligations (PRIV-7 bans shared logins). FR-22's field lists come from DOH/WHO research and are flagged for confirmation. The PRD makes no claims to innovation.

### Findings
- **low** PRIV-6 is an adjective, not a bound (§6 PRIV-6) — "let the hospital find out quickly who accessed or changed which records". *Fix:* state it as a capability, for example "an Admin can list all access to a given Patient Record, or all actions by a given Staff Account, over a date range, in one query."
- **low** NFR-3 has no availability target (§5 NFR-3) — "available during all OPD hours" has no allowed-downtime figure. The paper fallback is a good hedge, but architecture needs a number. *Fix:* add a target (for example, no more than N minutes of unplanned downtime per month during OPD hours).

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis holds. Every step of UJ-1 → UJ-2 → UJ-3 serves "fair, visible queues fed by fast triage, with records that persist", and the MVP is a problem-solving MVP. The counter-metrics are thoughtful: SM-C1 guards against gaming SM-1 by skipping Vital Signs, and SM-C2 guards against early No-shows.

The Success Metrics lean toward measuring activity. SM-2 ("100% of served patients have measured wait times") measures whether the system records data, not whether waits got shorter or fairer. The Vision promises "faster triage" and "fairer queues", but there is no baseline and no target for waiting time. The Doctors directory and duty schedule (FR-19, FR-20) connect only weakly to the thesis. They support only the optional Doctor on an Appointment and a "who's on duty" lookup.

### Findings
- **medium** No outcome metric for waiting time (§9 SM-1, SM-2) — SM-2 is about measurement coverage, and SM-1 has no pre-system baseline, so the claim "faster triage" cannot be checked. *Fix:* take a paper-process baseline during the pilot (§10), and add a target for median or 90th-percentile Queue-Entry-to-Serving time per Facility, or say explicitly that v1 only instruments and v2 sets targets.
- **medium** A single-Facility pilot conflicts with multi-Facility features (§10 vs FR-14, UJ-4, FR-16) — the pilot starts with Consultation only, but Referral and the "one screen showing all Facilities" (§11) assume every Facility is live. During the pilot it is undefined what happens when a patient is referred to a Facility that is not live. *Fix:* add a pilot-mode note: which features are active, and how Referral works to or from paper Facilities.
- **low** Directory scope is weakly justified (§4.8) — FR-19 and FR-20 are in MVP but none of the Primary or Secondary SMs covers them. *Fix:* either justify them (for example, Appointments with a Doctor are common for Prenatal), or reduce v1 to a Doctor name list and defer duty schedules.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs have sharp, testable consequences. Good examples are FR-9's "A Senior Citizen who arrives after three Routine patients is placed ahead of all three", FR-11's "Two staff pressing *Call next* at the same moment never get the same patient", FR-7's impossible-value bounds, and FR-16's no-PII rule. These are the strongest part of the PRD.

The gaps are concentrated in configuration and role permissions, and some edge rules are left undefined.

### Findings
- **high** A configurable Urgency scale breaks FR-9 ordering (FR-7 last bullet, §3 Urgency Level, FR-9) — "The Urgency Level scale can be configured by an Admin" (and 5-level ESI is mentioned), but FR-9 hard-codes "Urgent first … then Routine". If an Admin adds levels, nobody knows where they fall in the order or which level means "sent to ER". *Fix:* either lock v1 to the 3 levels (move configurability to v2), or define configuration as an ordered list of levels, each with a rank and a "directs to ER" flag, and restate FR-9 in terms of rank.
- **high** The Admin configuration surface has no FRs (FR-2 "manage … Facilities", FR-9 "enable or disable each Priority Category", FR-15 "configurable target", FR-17 "Facilities have opening days", FR-12 "closing time", §4.6 "registered display devices") — six Admin-editable settings are promised in passing, but only Doctors and Departments (FR-19) and accounts (FR-3) have FRs. Story creation will either miss these settings or invent them. *Fix:* add an FR group "4.10 OPD Settings" with one FR each for: Facility management (name, prefix, opening days and hours), Priority Category toggles, the Urgency scale (or its removal), the prenatal contact target and ABC schedule, and Display Board device registration and revocation. Give each testable consequences.
- **medium** Role permissions are incomplete (FR-2 vs FR-6, FR-14, FR-17, FR-21) — FR-2 covers only registration and triage, Queues, and admin entities. FR-6 lets "Staff" correct demographics, FR-17 lets "Staff" book Appointments, and FR-21 shows every Facility's numbers to all "signed-in staff", even though §4.1 says "Facility Staff see only their Facilities' Queues". *Fix:* add a role × action matrix to FR-2 that covers patient edit, Appointment booking and check-in, Referral, Overview visibility, and audit-log review.
- **medium** The timestamp that orders a Referral is ambiguous (FR-14, FR-9, UJ-4 assumption) — FR-9 orders within a group "by Queue Entry time", and a Referral "creates a Queue Entry", so the referred patient goes to the back of their group. UJ-4 says "not at the very end". FR-11 keeps the original entry time for restored No-shows, but FR-14 says nothing about referrals. *Fix:* state whether a referred entry is ordered by the referral time or by the original triage time.
- **medium** Backfilling paper visits has no FR (NFR-3) — "a way to enter paper-recorded visits afterwards" implies backdated Visits and Queue Entries. These affect Queue Number sequences (FR-8, FR-12), wait metrics (SM-2 "100%"), and the audit trail. *Fix:* add an FR for backfill entry (who can do it, how timestamps and Queue Numbers are handled, whether backfilled visits are excluded from SMs), or move it to v2 explicitly.
- **medium** FR-15's booster rule rests on an unverified source (FR-15, addendum Sources) — "For a previously immunized patient it suggests only the Day 3 booster" is stated as fact, but the addendum marks "The booster-only rule for previously immunized patients was *unverified* in the AO text." It is also unclear whether a Day 3 visit recomputes the Day 7 suggestion. *Fix:* tag it `[ASSUMPTION]`, fold it into the `[NOTE FOR PM]` for FR-22, and define how suggestions work on later doses.
- **low** The No-show threshold is undefined (UJ-2 edge case, FR-11) — "after an agreed number of calls" never becomes a number or a setting. *Fix:* add a default (for example, 3 recalls) and say whether the system enforces it or leaves it to staff judgment.
- **low** Thin or ambiguous consequences (FR-19, FR-21, FR-12) — FR-19 has no consequences at all, for example what happens to future Appointments when a Doctor is deactivated. For FR-21's "Average and longest current waiting time", it is unclear whether this is over patients still Waiting or patients already served. FR-12 handles only leftover Waiting entries, not Called or Serving entries at closing. *Fix:* add one consequence each.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§7 Non-Goals does real work (no EMR, no ER, no billing, no integration), §2.2 makes non-users explicit, and v2 deferrals are named in §8.2. The open-item load is heavy for a green-light PRD: 5 open questions, about 30 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, and 3 `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts. It is acceptable only because §11 says none of them block UX, architecture or epics, and that claim mostly holds. The exception is FR-22.

### Findings
- **high** FR-22 is both in and out of scope (FR-22, §8.1, §8.2 first bullet) — FR-22 specifies full ABC and Prenatal field sets as requirements, but §8.2 lists "Facility-specific clinical forms beyond basic fields: in scope only as far as §4.5's notes confirm", and §8.1 does not mention them. The epic planner cannot tell whether FR-22 is a v1 story. *Fix:* state plainly that FR-22 is in MVP, with its fields subject to confirmation by the facility heads, and rewrite the §8.2 bullet to exclude only fields beyond FR-22's list.
- **medium** The audit trail is in MVP scope but is not an FR (§8.1 "The audit trail", §6 PRIV-2) — it exists only as a privacy clause. "Admins can review the logs" has no screen, filters, or consequences, and "every view of a Patient Record" is logged, but it is unclear whether a queue list showing names counts as a view. *Fix:* promote it to an FR (logging scope plus an Admin review capability), and tie it to PRIV-6.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This PRD is chain-top (§0 names `bmad-ux`, `bmad-architecture` and `bmad-create-epics-and-stories`), so this dimension matters. The Glossary is strong, and most FRs use its terms with capitals. Every FR has "Realizes UJ-n" back-links, and every SM names the FRs it validates. The addendum keeps technical reuse out of the requirements, and the brownfield file references (`orders-query.ts`, `orders-href.ts`, `orders-toolbar.tsx`, `orders-pagination.tsx`, `nav-items.ts`, `src/lib/data.ts`, `globals.css`) all resolve in `src/`. The problems are the config and audit gaps above, which extraction will miss because they have no FR IDs, plus some state-vocabulary drift (see Mechanical notes).

### Findings
- **medium** Visit and Queue Entry state vocabularies don't line up (§3 Visit, §3 Queue Status, FR-8, FR-13) — a Visit ends as "Completed, Referred-and-completed, No-show or Cancelled". FR-8 adds an unlisted end, "recorded as directed to the Emergency Room". Queue Status uses "Done" while FR-13 says "complete the Queue Entry". The data model and UX will each guess the mapping. *Fix:* add "Directed to ER" to the Visit end states, use one term (Done or Completed) for Queue Entries, and add a short Visit-state ↔ Queue-Status mapping to §3.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a multi-role internal tool with a public-facing screen and a brownfield codebase. Grouping by feature with nested FRs fits, and the six UJs are the right number: three full journeys for the core loop and three short ones for variants. Brownfield handling is accurate. §4.2 reuses the story 1.4 table pattern, §4.9 keeps the story 1.5 shell, and the addendum flags the known toolbar-sync bug and the story 1.3 that was never built. Regulatory traceability (§6, FR-9 legal basis) fits a system that handles sensitive health data. The PRD is neither over- nor under-formalized.

## Mechanical notes
- **FR ordering:** FR-22 sits between FR-15 and FR-16 (§4.5). IDs 1–22 are unique and complete but not in document order. FR-13's "(see Notes)" and §8.2's "§4.5's notes" point to the Notes block under FR-22. Consider renumbering, or pointing to "FR-22".
- **UJ protagonists:** UJ-4 ("A patient is referred…") and UJ-5 ("A returning prenatal patient…") have no named protagonist, unlike UJ-1, 2, 3 and 6.
- **Glossary drift:** "Staff" (generic, in FR-4, FR-6, FR-17) vs the Role "Facility Staff". "group" (FR-9, FR-10) and "Routine" used as a group name are not defined in §3. "closing time" (FR-12) vs "opening days" (FR-17) vs "OPD hours" (NFR-3) name the same concept with no Glossary entry.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:**
  - Index entries with no inline tag: §3 "The 3-level Urgency Level", §4.4 "priority ordering policy (strict vs. alternating)" (this is actually a decision, not an assumption), §4.5 "suggested follow-up dates", §6 "Philippines and RA 10173".
  - Inline tags that §11 has since resolved but that are still marked as assumptions: §3 Hospital Number ("no existing scheme" is resolved in §11), FR-8 printing hardware (resolved in §11 as "browser print dialog").
- **Resolved vs open:** §11 item 1 and item 3 duplicate §12 entries that claim to be confirmed (see Decision-readiness).
- **Frontmatter title** "Hospital Management System — PRD" vs H1 "Hospital OPD Management System" (marked "Working title — confirm").
